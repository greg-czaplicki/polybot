# Polywhaler — Design Context

_Last updated: 2026-04-22_

## Design Context

### Users

Single operator (the product owner) on both phone and desktop. No collaborators, no public surface — the app is password-gated by `APP_PASSWORD`. Every design choice gets to be **opinionated**; there is no broad-audience trade-off to make. Optimize for one person using the tool intensely.

**Primary contexts:**
- **Desktop / research mode** — extended sessions tuning the grading model, reviewing ranked opportunities, inspecting bot & whale candidates, scanning top-holder PnL. Large monitor, long reads, dense columns, many tabs open.
- **Phone / live mode** — quick reads during live games to confirm the market is moving the way signals say it should. Installed as a PWA (`display: standalone`, portrait-primary). One-handed use on the couch, at a bar, in bed at 11pm.

The phone view is not a reduced desktop view. It is the same tool at a different density — information reshapes, never gets amputated.

### The Job To Be Done

Help a single sharp bettor act on Polymarket sports markets with confidence, using: sharp money flow, edge rating, signal score and grade bands (A+/A/B/…), bot & whale candidate identification, top-holder PnL tracking, canonical matchups, ranked opportunities with priceEdge, and runtime market stats.

The interface exists to support **decisions**. "Does this market still have edge?" "Is the sharp signal holding?" "Is this whale following their usual pattern?" The answer must be readable in under three seconds on a phone and scannable across columns on a desktop.

### Brand Personality

**Sharp · numerical · quietly confident**

- **Sharp** is literal and metaphorical — the core product concept (sharp money) and the visual character (precise edges, tight alignment, nothing fuzzy, no decoration that doesn't earn its place).
- **Numerical** — the substance is numbers. Typography, alignment, and layout serve the numbers. Tabular figures, aligned columns, mono where columns need to scan.
- **Quietly confident** — not a trading-terminal light show, not a screaming sports app. Feel like a precision instrument — a pilot's altimeter, a harbormaster's tide chart — comfortable in its own competence, no need to perform.

**Emotional goal:** the user should feel *sharper* after opening the app. Less noise, clearer signal, more confident decisions.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Reference — OddsJam**, for its attitude: dense, analyst-first, tabular, shows real data without cosmetic softening. Polywhaler should feel like that kind of professional tool, but better composed and more restrained — less affiliate-site clutter, less random color coding, less sales chrome. Closer to what OddsJam would look like if it were designed for a single serious operator rather than a subscription funnel.

**Anti-references (explicitly not this):**
- Consumer sports-app aesthetic — team logos splashed across cards, score-ticker decoration, stadium imagery.
- Generic shadcn dashboard look — zinc palette, equal card grids, icon-above-heading templated pages. (This is where the project sits today, by default, not by design.)
- Trading-terminal clichés used decoratively — neon green/red tickers, Bloomberg-orange everywhere, CRT scanlines.
- The banned AI-slop surface list: glassmorphism, glow borders, gradient text, colored side-stripe accents on cards, decorative sparklines, rounded rectangles with generic drop shadows.

**Theme — dark only.** Used on phones late at night and desktops during games. A light theme would only exist to play it safe. Commit. Don't use flat gray neutrals — tint surfaces toward the brand navy so the base has a subconscious relationship to the logo.

### Brand Palette (derived from the whale logo)

- **Navy** `#122a4b` ≈ `oklch(0.24 0.08 255)` — foundation. Tint all dark surfaces toward this hue (chroma 0.005–0.02 depending on depth). This replaces the current cold zinc `#111827`.
- **Mid-blue** `#6488d3` ≈ `oklch(0.62 0.12 260)` — primary interactive. Links, selected states, rest of the hover/focus system.
- **Cyan** `#57c6e4` ≈ `oklch(0.78 0.10 215)` — accent. **Rare.** Reserved for peaks in the visual hierarchy — a top-grade badge, a confirmed sharp signal, the one thing on the screen that deserves attention. Never decorative. Never as a glowing drop-shadow.
- **Neutrals** — all tinted toward navy, never neutral gray. Text, borders, surfaces sit on this hue axis.
- **Semantic colors** (positive/negative for PnL, grade bands, edge movement) — derived and tinted toward the brand palette, not out-of-the-box red/green (which reads as consumer sports).

### Typography

- **Numbers are the primary typographic object.** The numeric face must have excellent tabular figures (`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`) and be genuinely distinctive — avoid IBM Plex Mono, JetBrains Mono (reads generic at this point), Space Mono.
- **UI chrome** (labels, headings, buttons, body) — a humanist or neo-grotesk sans with a tight character width. Avoid Inter, DM Sans, Plus Jakarta, Outfit, Instrument Sans, and anything else on the reflex-reject list.
- **Fixed rem scales** inside the product UI — no clamp-based fluid type in the app itself. Fluid type belongs on marketing pages, which Polywhaler does not have.
- **Hierarchy should be dense**, not airy — compact headings, tight metric labels. This is a dashboard, not an essay. Pair with at least a 1.25 ratio between steps.

### Motion

Restrained. Motion is only used to (a) orient the user across state changes (filters applying, data refreshing, navigating between markets) and (b) acknowledge user action. No decorative animations, no entrance choreography, no staggered card reveals. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` always.

### Density

Lean dense over airy. Analyst tools feel wrong when they waste space. Tighter spacing tokens at the component level; more generous spacing only at page-section boundaries (create rhythm through contrast, not uniformity). Embrace tables, embrace data.

### Accessibility

- Target **WCAG AA**.
- All text must meet 4.5:1 on its surface. Small numeric labels on navy-tinted dark surfaces are the risk area — test contrast every time.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` respected on every transition.
- Focus states visible and navy-derived, not the default browser blue ring.

## Design Principles

1. **Every pixel of chrome costs signal.** If a decoration doesn't help a decision, remove it. When in doubt, remove again.
2. **The numbers are the interface.** Typography, alignment, and layout serve the data. Tabular figures, aligned columns, mono where columns need to scan.
3. **Tint toward the whale.** Dark surfaces, borders, and muted text all sit on the navy hue axis — never neutral gray. This creates subconscious cohesion with the logo.
4. **Cyan is rare.** The brand's brightest color belongs to the one thing on the screen that matters most. Overuse kills its signal value.
5. **Phone and desktop are the same tool.** The phone view reshapes, never amputates. Container queries, reflowing tables, density that adapts rather than features that disappear.
