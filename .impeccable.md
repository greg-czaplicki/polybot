# Polywhaler — Design Context

_Last updated: 2026-09-03_

## Design Context

_Rewritten 2026-09-03: the product is now a fully automated book. The operator no longer picks bets; the app is the terminal that supervises the machine._

### Users

Single operator (the product owner), desktop and phone with equal weight. Password-gated, no collaborators. Every choice may be opinionated.

**What changed since April:** the bot on the VPS places every pick. The operator's job is now **supervision, not selection** — is the machine alive, how is the book doing, is anything waiting on a pre-registered decision. Research screens (holder flow, runtime stats, canonical games, wallets) are opened rarely and belong in a Research area, not the top nav.

**Contexts:**
- **Desktop / terminal mode** — one dense multi-panel workspace, open on a second monitor during a slate. Panels are tiled, not stacked; drilling in should not mean leaving the screen.
- **Phone / glance mode** — PWA, one-handed, late night. The same panels reflow to a single column, with the alive/book strip pinned first. Reshape, never amputate.

### The Job To Be Done

Answer, within three seconds of opening:
1. **Is the machine alive?** Bot heartbeat, sync health, Pinnacle credit budget, last pick, staleness alarms.
2. **How is the book doing?** Bankroll, open positions, realized P&L and ROI by window, CLV, stake-ladder state.

Second-tier, still on the terminal but below the fold or in side panels:
3. **Does anything need my decision?** Pre-registered verdicts per market (HOLD / WATCH / READY), reads coming due, incidents.
4. **What is the tape doing?** Per-market (sport) flow: live and shadow volume, pin anchors captured, rejects by gate, upcoming slate.

### Brand Personality

**Sharp · numerical · quietly confident** (unchanged). The terminal feels like a precision instrument, not a light show.

### Aesthetic Direction

**Reference — a Bloomberg workspace, for its *structure*, not its costume.** Tiled panels on one screen, each with a terse uppercase title bar, tabular monospace numerics, tight row height, no card chrome, keyboard-scannable. What we take: the multi-panel workspace and the tape density. What we explicitly do not take: black-and-amber colorway, function-key hints, CRT effects, scrolling tickers. The existing navy palette and Hanken Grotesk / Red Hat Mono pairing stay.

**Panel grammar:**
- A panel is a titled region separated by 1px navy-tinted hairlines, never a rounded card with a shadow. Panels tile edge-to-edge on desktop and stack on phone.
- Panel title bar: 11px uppercase tracked label, left; freshness stamp or count, right. Nothing else.
- Status is carried by color on the value itself (positive / warn / bad hues), never by side-stripes, badges-everywhere, or background floods. One dominant status word per panel at most.
- Numbers right-align in Red Hat Mono with tabular figures. Labels are Hanken Grotesk.
- Long pre-registered reads (the old /shadow) become a **verdict board**: one row per market with the verdict, the sole blocker, n / z / CLV, and the next checkpoint. Detail opens inline, never as a wall of tables.

**Anti-references (still true):** consumer sports-app decoration, generic shadcn card grids, terminal clichés used as costume, and the banned AI-slop surface list (glassmorphism, glow borders, gradient text, colored side-stripes, decorative sparklines, rounded rectangles with generic drop shadows).

**Theme — dark only.** Unchanged.

### Brand Palette (derived from the whale logo)

- **Navy** `#122a4b` ≈ `oklch(0.24 0.08 255)` — foundation. Tint all dark surfaces toward this hue (chroma 0.005–0.02 depending on depth). This replaces the current cold zinc `#111827`.
- **Mid-blue** `#6488d3` ≈ `oklch(0.62 0.12 260)` — primary interactive. Links, selected states, rest of the hover/focus system.
- **Cyan** `#57c6e4` ≈ `oklch(0.78 0.10 215)` — accent. **Rare.** Reserved for peaks in the visual hierarchy — a top-grade badge, a confirmed sharp signal, the one thing on the screen that deserves attention. Never decorative. Never as a glowing drop-shadow.
- **Neutrals** — all tinted toward navy, never neutral gray. Text, borders, surfaces sit on this hue axis.
- **Semantic colors** (positive/negative for PnL, grade bands, edge movement) — derived and tinted toward the brand palette, not out-of-the-box red/green (which reads as consumer sports).

### Typography

- **Numbers are the primary typographic object.** The numeric face must have excellent tabular figures (`font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums`) and be genuinely distinctive — avoid IBM Plex Mono, JetBrains Mono (reads generic at this point), Space Mono.
- **UI chrome** (labels, headings, buttons, body) — a humanist or neo-grotesk sans with a tight character width. Avoid Inter, DM Sans, Plus Jakarta, Outfit, Instrument Sans, and anything else on the reflex-reject list.
- **Fixed rem scales** inside the product UI — no clamp-based fluid type in the app itself. Fluid type belongs on marketing pages, which Polywhaler does not have.
- **Hierarchy should be dense**, not airy — compact headings, tight metric labels. This is a dashboard, not an essay. Pair with at least a 1.25 ratio between steps.

### Motion

Restrained. Motion is only used to (a) orient the user across state changes (filters applying, data refreshing, navigating between markets) and (b) acknowledge user action. No decorative animations, no entrance choreography, no staggered card reveals. Respect `prefers-reduced-motion` always.

### Density

Lean dense over airy. Analyst tools feel wrong when they waste space. Tighter spacing tokens at the component level; more generous spacing only at page-section boundaries (create rhythm through contrast, not uniformity). Embrace tables, embrace data.

### Accessibility

- Target **WCAG AA**.
- All text must meet 4.5:1 on its surface. Small numeric labels on navy-tinted dark surfaces are the risk area — test contrast every time.
- `prefers-reduced-motion` respected on every transition.
- Focus states visible and navy-derived, not the default browser blue ring.

## Design Principles

1. **Every pixel of chrome costs signal.** If a decoration doesn't help a decision, remove it. When in doubt, remove again.
2. **The numbers are the interface.** Typography, alignment, and layout serve the data. Tabular figures, aligned columns, mono where columns need to scan.
3. **Tint toward the whale.** Dark surfaces, borders, and muted text all sit on the navy hue axis — never neutral gray. This creates subconscious cohesion with the logo.
4. **Cyan is rare.** The brand's brightest color belongs to the one thing on the screen that matters most. Overuse kills its signal value.
5. **Phone and desktop are the same tool.** The phone view reshapes, never amputates. Container queries, reflowing tables, density that adapts rather than features that disappear.
