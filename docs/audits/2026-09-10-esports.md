# Esports — first read from the tape (2026-09-10)

Esports entered the shadow book today (era v13). The tape already holds
1,111 settled esports match-winner markets (June–September) and the book
recorder ~150 esports markets since 9/9. Scripts: `research/sharp/esports_size.py`,
`esports_edge.py`.

## Findings
1. **Favourites are overpriced, dogs underpriced** (T−60m mid, 1,059 markets):
   0.55–0.70 implied 62% actual 58%; 0.70–0.85: 76% vs 72%; 0.85–0.97: 90% vs
   84%. Backing every dog < 0.45 at MID: +10% (z 1.7, n=862 train). At the
   taker price (mid + 3c in a 6c book): ≈ 0. Dog CLV +0.3c (z 1.5); deep dogs
   < 0.15 +1.7c (z 2.5). The bias is real at mid and eaten by the spread.
   Test half is only 22 markets (our tracked universe had almost no esports
   before 9/9) — this needs the forward read.
2. **Pregame maker economics hold in esports**: realized half-spread +0.4c
   per fill (CS2 z 22 on 127k fills; Dota, Valorant same), adverse selection
   0.1–0.3c. Unlike MLB, the queue at the touch is $3.5k with a 6c spread,
   so improving the bid by a tick is possible and cheap.
3. **Esports-specialist wallets**: nothing (test n=6; dead on arrival like
   every wallet-ranking test).

## What the edge would look like
Not a taker strategy. A passive dog bid: rest a bid on the underdog at or
inside the mid, in CS2 first, sized small, cancelled at T−15m. It combines
the two positive findings (dog mispricing at mid, maker spread capture) in
the one market where our size is not a disadvantage. Fill rate and realised
P&L are measurable on the recorder now; the paper MM restricted to esports
is the first check, a one-sided dog-only variant the second.
